# A uniform omission schedule that stops short of a cycle construction

The fixed-hub certificates through `h=19` suggest a uniform way to decide which **leaves** a prospective cycle omits. This is an incidence lemma, not an edge decomposition of `K_{h,t}`.

Let `h=2k+1≥5`, choose `1≤j≤k−1`, set `t=h+2j=2(k+j)+1`, and propose `r=k+j` cycles: `k` full cycles visiting all `h` hubs and `j` short cycles visiting `h−1` hubs. On a tape of `jt` positions, concatenate `k` blocks of length `2j` followed by `j` blocks of length `2j+1`. Map tape position `p` to leaf `p mod t`, and let each block list the leaves **omitted** by its prospective cycle.

The block lengths sum to `k(2j)+j(2j+1)=j(2k+2j+1)=jt`. Each length is less than `t`, so a block has distinct leaves. The tape runs through `j` whole laps of `Z_t`, so each leaf is omitted exactly `j` times and is active in exactly `r−j=k` prospective cycles. A length-`2j` block leaves `t−2j=h` active leaves, and a length-`2j+1` block leaves `h−1`. This proves the leaf-incidence counts for every allowed `k,j`.

Give singleton spokes from consecutive leaf triples `{3a,3a+1,3a+2}` to hub `a` for `a=0,…,j−1`; assign the remaining `h−j` leaves one apiece to hubs `j,…,h−1` in order. Have short cycle `k+a` omit hub `a`. A triple-spoke hub then needs degree `t−3=2(r−1)` in the cycles and is scheduled into `r−1` cycles; every other hub needs degree `t−1=2r` and is scheduled into all `r`. Each leaf needs degree `h−1=2k` and is scheduled into `k` cycles. Thus **every vertex has the right cycle incidence degree**. There remains a nontrivial problem: choose, for each color, alternating hub–leaf edges that form one simple cycle, with each spoke used once. The degree accounting alone gives neither edge-disjoint 2-factors nor connected color classes.

One tempting stronger template already fails. Suppose all leaves whose singleton spoke meets hub `a` use the *same* perfect matching `M_a` on the other `2k` hubs to pair their cycle edges. A simple cycle can include at most `k` such leaves because it can use each matching edge once. If the cycle omits a different hub `b`, only `k−1` edges of `M_a` avoid `b`, so it can include at most `k−1` of them. At `h=5,j=1,t=7`, the interval short-cycle block omits leaves `{4,5,6}` but includes leaves `{0,1,2}`, all three of which have singleton hub 0; `3>k=2`. At `h=7,j=2,t=11`, the second short cycle omits hub 1 and includes all three hub-0 singleton leaves `{0,1,2}`; `3>k−1=2`. The fixed-`M_a` template cannot realize these interval schedules. These are **not** counterexamples to the odd-hub formula: the certified graph decompositions exist at both sizes, and leaf-specific pairings can avoid the obstruction.

The finite `h=19` [JSON certificates](32_nineteen_hub_odd_seeds.json) use this schedule for their omission sets; the [checker](32_nineteen_hub_all_residues.py) verifies the actual edges independently of the incidence argument. A uniform edge-assignment or switching proof is still needed before the [general conjecture](27_odd_hub_design_question.md) can become a theorem. Publication priority of this particular schedule is unverified.
