# The exact four-way terminal law on eight vertices

**Source and scope.** This extends [item 11's small-order census](11_triangle_removal_small.md) for the process in family 188's [triangle-removal paper](../../preprints/The-Sharp-Terminal-Leave-in-Random-Triangle-Removal-September-25-2026/build/introduction.tex): start from `K_n`, choose uniformly among *currently present* triangles, remove their edges, and stop when no triangle remains. The paper's theorem concerns the large-`n` leading constant. Here `n=8` is fixed, and all probabilities below are finite exact rational values.

| Terminal leave from `K₈` | Edges `F₈` | Labelled terminal graphs | Exact probability |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Perfect matching | 4 | 105 | `599217/3203200` ≈ 18.707% |
| Seven-edge star `K_{1,7}` | 7 | 8 | `1359/553280` ≈ 0.246% |
| Two-center tree, center degrees 5 and 3 | 7 | 840 | `570231/3477760` ≈ 16.397% |
| Three-center tree, center degrees 3, 3, 3 | 7 | 5,040 | `78694289/121721600` ≈ 64.651% |

The four probabilities add to one. The [diagram](art/triangle_removal_eight.svg) shows them on a common scale; the star is marked by a thin line because its probability is below a quarter of one percent. There are 5,993 reachable labelled terminal masks in total. The process removes either eight triangles and leaves four edges, or seven triangles and leaves seven edges.

There is also a **structural proof that no other terminal edge count occurs**, independent of the state search. Removing a triangle lowers three vertex degrees by two, so every terminal degree remains odd as it was in `K₈`. The number of remaining edges is `28−3j`, hence congruent to one modulo three. All eight degrees are at least one, so there are at least four edges.

Let `H` be any triangle-free eight-vertex graph with every degree odd. If it has a degree-seven vertex, it is the seven-edge star. If its maximum degree is five, take a degree-five vertex `v`. Its five neighbors form an independent set `A`; the other two vertices form `B`. Each vertex of `A` meets either zero or both vertices of `B`, since it already has one edge to `v` and its degree is odd. If the two vertices of `B` are adjacent, no vertex of `A` can meet both, or there would be a triangle, so `H` has exactly six edges. If they are not adjacent, say `a` vertices of `A` meet both; each vertex of `B` has degree `a`, which must be odd. Thus `a∈{1,3,5}` and `H` has `5+2a∈{7,11,15}` edges. None is ten or thirteen. If the maximum degree is three, let `a` vertices have degree three and the other `8−a` have degree one. Then `|E(H)|=4+a≤12`. Combining this with the congruence leaves only **4, 7, or 10** edges to consider.

Ten edges would force degree sequence `3⁶,1²`. In fact, there are only two triangle-free shapes with that sequence. If the two degree-one vertices are adjacent, the remaining six vertices form a triangle-free cubic graph on six vertices, necessarily `K_{3,3}`: the three neighbors of any vertex are independent and must each join the other two vertices to reach degree three. If the degree-one vertices are not adjacent, delete them and consider their two neighbors in the six-vertex core. The core has eight edges and is triangle-free. It must be bipartite: a nonbipartite triangle-free six-vertex graph contains a five-cycle, and its sixth vertex has at most two neighbors on that cycle, giving at most seven edges. A bipartite six-vertex graph with eight edges is either `K_{2,4}` or `K_{3,3}` minus one edge. The core degree sequence rules out `K_{2,4}` and rules out both degree-one vertices attaching to the same core vertex. Thus the core is `K_{3,3}−xy`, with the two pendant edges attached at `x` and `y`.

Neither ten-edge shape can be a terminal leave of this process. The removed-edge complement `K₈−H` would have to split into six triangles. For `H=K_{3,3} ⊔ K₂`, each of the two degree-one vertices is adjacent in the complement to all three vertices of either side of `K_{3,3}`, but those three incident edges can only occur in pairs in complement triangles: the two sides have no cross edges in the complement, and the two degree-one vertices have no complement edge between them. An odd three cannot be paired. For the pendant shape, the missing core edge `xy` is in the complement but belongs to no complement triangle: every other core vertex lies on one side of the bipartition and misses one of `x,y`, while each pendant vertex misses its attachment endpoint. So no triangle partition exists. This excludes ten edges and proves that terminal leaves have only four or seven edges. There are `28·(10+180)=5,320` labelled ten-edge candidate leaves in those two shapes, all excluded by these local obstructions. [`12_triangle_removal_obstructions.py`](12_triangle_removal_obstructions.py) independently enumerates the 190 candidates for a fixed pair of degree-one vertices and checks both obstructions; the structural proof does not depend on that enumeration.

The four possible terminal degree sequences then follow without a search: `1⁸` for four edges, or `7,1⁷`, `5,3,1⁶`, and `3³,1⁵` for seven edges. Four edges with all degrees odd force a perfect matching. With seven edges, the first sequence is a star. In the `5,3,1⁶` case, six leaf edges leave one edge joining the two high-degree vertices. In the `3³,1⁵` case, five leaf edges leave two edges among the three high-degree vertices, necessarily a path; its middle vertex has one attached leaf and its endpoints have two each. Thus the three seven-edge shapes are trees. Their labelled counts also follow from choosing the centers and distributing the leaves: `8`, `8·7·binom(6,4)=840`, and `8·binom(7,2)·5·binom(4,2)=5040`.

Each shape really is reachable, too. In the table, a two-digit word is an edge of a representative terminal graph `H` on vertices `0,…,7`, and a three-digit word is a triangle. The triangles in each row partition **every edge of `K₈` outside `H`**; deleting them in any order reaches `H`. Relabelling covers every labelled copy of the shape.

| Shape | Edges of `H` | Triangles to delete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perfect matching | `01 23 45 67` | `034 027 056 125 246 147 357 136` |
| Star | `01 02 03 04 05 06 07` | `134 245 467 126 237 157 356` |
| Two-center tree | `01 02 03 04 05 16 17` | `067 123 145 246 347 257 356` |
| Three-center tree | `01 12 03 04 15 26 27` | `025 067 234 137 146 457 356` |

The companion obstruction script checks the exact edge coverage of these four witness rows. Therefore the shape classification and the labelled terminal-mask counts are structural; the rational probabilities still require the process calculation.

[`12_triangle_removal_eight.py`](12_triangle_removal_eight.py) evaluates the exact terminal-shape recursion with `Fraction`, memoizing only masks reached from the complete graph. For a nonterminal mask `M` it averages the child laws over all triangles present in `M`; for a terminal mask it identifies the shape by its degree sequence. It visits **196,870 reachable labelled masks**, verifies all four probabilities and terminal-mask counts, and generates the diagram. Run `python3 -I playground/marlow/12_triangle_removal_eight.py` from the repository root. This is an exact small-graph distribution, not an asymptotic estimate or an audit of the paper's `L²` theorem. I have not checked publication priority for these finite fractions, and I make no novelty claim.
