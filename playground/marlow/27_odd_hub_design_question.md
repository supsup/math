# The odd-base design question

The proved fixed cases through 27 hubs suggest the following **conjecture**, not a theorem of this note:

> For every odd `h=2k+1≥3` and every `t≥h−1`, the minimum cycle/singleton partition size of `K_{h,t}` is `d(K_{h,t})=t+ceil(kt/h)`.

The parity/cycle-capacity argument supplies this lower bound for every odd `h`; the same mechanism appears in [Bucić–Montgomery, Section 6](https://rhmontgomery.warwick.ac.uk/papers/28cyclepacking.pdf) as the Erdős/Gallai bipartite extremal example. [Sotteau's theorem or the explicit even-hub weave](24_even_base_residues.md) supplies every even base `b∈[h−1,2h−2]`, while the offset construction supplies `b=h`. Adding an `h`-leaf offset block preserves equality. Thus one needs only `k−1` odd bases, `b=h+2j` for `1≤j≤k−1`, to prove the conjecture for a given `h`. [Items 20, 23, 25, 26, 29, 31, 32, 37, 38, and 40](README.md) store finite hub proofs through `h=27`; `h=3,5` are in the earlier playground notes. [Item 34](34_prime_two_leaf_growth.md) also proves every prime `h≡3 (mod 4)`. [Item 42](42_twentynine_affine_growth.md) proves `h=29` by compatible affine growth pages. The first unverified odd hub count is composite `h=33`, starting with `t=35`.

Here is a sharper **sufficient design problem** for each missing base. Set `t=h+2j`. Choose one singleton spoke at every leaf so that `j` hubs receive three singleton spokes and the other `h−j` hubs receive one. Decompose the remaining graph into exactly `k` full cycles of length `2h` and `j` short cycles of length `2(h−1)`, with each short cycle omitting a different triple-spoke hub. The proposed cycle counts meet all hub degrees: a triple-spoke hub appears in `k+j−1` cycles, and every other hub appears in all `k+j`. They also meet the global edge count because `h(k+j)−kt=j`, exactly the total of `j` omitted hub visits. The saved certificates for `h=9,11,13,15,17,19,21,25,27` exhibit this pattern. This is sufficient for the formula; it is not claimed necessary for every optimum.

Some incidence arithmetic *is* necessary in any optimal partition at this base. The lower-bound argument forces exactly one singleton at each leaf, hence each leaf appears in exactly `k` of the `r=k+j` cycles and is omitted from exactly `j`. At a hub `u`, the number of singleton spokes must be odd, at least one, and can be written `1+2a_u`. Since there are `t=h+2j` singleton spokes altogether, `sum_u a_u=j`. Hub `u` appears in exactly `r−a_u` cycles, so `a_u` is its number of missed cycles. The proposed design chooses `a_u=1` at `j` hubs and zero elsewhere, and puts each missing hub visit in a separate short cycle; an optimum could distribute those misses differently.

For `j=1`, the proposed **cycle-length and hub-singleton pattern is forced by any optimum**: `sum_u a_u=1` gives one triple-spoke hub and every other hub a single spoke; exactly one hub visit is missed, so precisely one cycle has length `2(h−1)` and the other `k` cycles have length `2h`. The short cycle must omit the triple-spoke hub. Every leaf participates in exactly `k` of the `k+1` cycles, so each leaf is omitted once. The `k` full cycles each omit two leaves, and the one short cycle omits three: these missing-leaf sets partition all `h+2=2k+3` leaves. A possible extra search constraint is to make the short-cycle omitted triple equal the three leaves whose singleton spokes meet the triple-spoke hub. The current certificates do not enforce that constraint, so its feasibility remains unproved. This reduces the search to a constrained near-complete bipartite Hamilton-cycle design, though no general construction is established here.

There is an equivalent **edge-colored near-one-factor view** of this `j=1` case. Contract each leaf's two incident edges *within a cycle* to an edge joining its two hub neighbors, labeled by that leaf and colored by the cycle. Different leaves may label parallel hub pairs in the resulting multigraph. Across all cycles containing a fixed leaf, these `k` hub pairs form a perfect matching on the `2k` hubs other than its singleton neighbor; no hub can occur twice because every remaining spoke is used once. Each of the `k` full colors forms a Hamilton cycle on the `h` hubs with two leaf labels absent. The short color forms a Hamilton cycle on the `h−1` hubs other than the triple-spoke hub, with three leaf labels absent. Conversely, such colored matchings with distinct leaf labels within each colored cycle expand back to the desired bipartite decomposition. This reformulation may be more useful for a rotational construction than direct spoke-level search; it is a restatement, not a proof of existence.

There is a useful published benchmark for the **`j=0`** square seed. A [Kotzig factorization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0012365X83901565) combines a near-one-factorization and Hamilton decomposition of `K_h` so that each Hamilton cycle uses one edge from each near-one-factor; [Horton proved existence at every odd order](https://www-sop.inria.fr/members/Jean-Claude.Bermond/PUBLIS/ABS90.pdf). Labeling the near-one-factors by leaves and expanding their edges gives an alternative optimal `K_{h,h}` seed. Our offset seed need not be a Kotzig factorization: its contracted hub pairs may repeat across leaves. The theorem does not immediately add the two leaves or the short cycle required at `j=1`.

**Prior-art boundary.** Sotteau's fixed-length result applies after removing an entire hub at the even bases. [Horsley's theorem on arbitrary even-cycle lists](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.3368) addresses complete bipartite graphs with both sides even, or balanced odd sides after deleting a perfect matching; its stated hypotheses do not directly cover these unequal odd sides with the prescribed spoke deletion. This scope comparison does not prove the design problem new. A literature search for near-complete bipartite cycle decompositions with the specified missing stars is still needed before any novelty claim.
