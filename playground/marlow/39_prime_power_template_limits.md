# Two limits of the affine prime-power template

[Item 34](34_prime_two_leaf_growth.md) uses a translation-invariant Hamilton decomposition of `K_p` and translated primitive-power near-Hamilton cycles when the hub count is prime `p≡3 (mod 4)`. Two short arguments explain why directly copying both ingredients to other finite fields fails. They are **template limits**, not counterexamples to the [odd-hub formula](27_odd_hub_design_question.md). In particular, [item 38](38_twentyfive_hub_all_residues.md) proves the formula for `h=25` by finite certificates.

**No translation-invariant Hamilton seed over a nonprime finite field.** Let `q=p^r` be an odd prime power with `r>1` and `G=(F_q,+)≅C_p^r`. Suppose a Hamilton decomposition of `K_q` were permuted by every translation in `G`. It would contain `(q−1)/2` Hamilton cycles. The `G`-orbits on these cycles have sizes powers of `p`; because `(q−1)/2` is not divisible by `p`, at least one cycle is fixed by all translations. The faithful regular action of `G` on its vertices would then embed `G` in the automorphism group `D_{2q}` of that `q`-cycle. Every `p`-subgroup of `D_{2q}` lies in its cyclic rotation subgroup `C_q`, but `C_p^r` is noncyclic. This is impossible. The result rules out a fully translation-equivariant Hamilton color seed, not an arbitrary Kotzig square or a different finite-field construction.

**Parity obstruction for *all* primitive-power pages at `q≡1 (mod 4)`.** In any odd finite field `F_q`, take a primitive `g` and the near-Hamilton hub cycles `Q_a=(a+g^i)_{i=0}^{q−2}`, one for each `a∈F_q`. As in the [classical orthogonal double cover](https://www.math.uni-luebeck.de/mitarbeiter/bey/pub/orthogonal_double_covers_by_super-extendable_cycles__02_BEY_HARTMANN_LECK_LECK.pdf), every hub edge lies in exactly two pages. Put `k=(q−1)/2`. The edge `(a+u,a+gu)` at index `i` also appears, in reverse, in the page centered at `b=a+(1+g)u` at index `i+k`: indeed `g^k=−1`. If `q≡1 (mod 4)`, then `k` is even, so the two occurrences have the **same alternating parity**.

Suppose a fixed `k`-coloring of `E(K_q)` made every page's even-indexed matching rainbow. A given color would appear once in each of the `q` pages, yielding an odd `q` page incidences. Every edge of that color contributes either zero or two even-page incidences, yielding an even total. Contradiction. Thus no edge coloring makes **all `q` pages** simultaneously rainbow. [Item 35](35_compatible_growth_criterion.md) only asks for `k−1` selected pages with further label conditions, so this parity count does not rule out its criterion at `q=25` or at primes `1 (mod 4)`.

The [field checker](39_prime_power_template_probe.py) represents `F_25` as `F_5[x]/(x²+2)` and verifies the facts for a primitive generator: all 300 hub edges occur twice with the same parity, all 300 page pairs share one edge, and each page has 24 distinct affine midpoint labels. The [diagram](art/prime_power_template_limits.svg) shows the two counting contradictions. The group and parity proofs above hold for the stated prime-power classes; the script is a finite backstop, not their proof. Priority of these particular observations is unverified.
