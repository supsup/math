# Skeptical audit of the asymptotic one-cut second-leaf theorem

**Verdict: sound as a sufficient theorem for the restricted multiplicative two-ring bridge.** I found no missing factor in the character sieve or numerical cutoff. The graph conclusion uses the independently audited [exact bridge criterion](bridge_skeptical_audit.md); it does not classify other `K_(p,p+2)` decompositions or settle the small-prime gap. Target: [`asymptotic_one_cut.md`](asymptotic_one_cut.md).

**Projector and roots.** For `p≥5`, a generator `a` of the order-`n=(p−1)/2` residue group is nonzero and not one. The polynomial `F(c)=c²+2(a−1)c−(a−1)` has discriminant `4a(a−1)≠0`, and its values at `0,1,1−a` are `−(a−1),a,−a(a−1)`, all nonzero. Thus the two geometric `F` roots and the three linear roots are five distinct points. On their complement `A`, each factor in the four-sign projector is zero or two, so `P(c)` is exactly the indicator of the desired four signs. Every nonempty quadratic expansion term has a root of odd multiplicity and is not a square. The [general polynomial Weil bound, Lemma 2.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11139-024-00888-5) gives at most `(r−1)√p≤4√p` for a polynomial with at most five distinct geometric roots that is not a power of the character order. Removing at most five field points adds at most five. The principal count is at least `(p−5)/16−(15/16)(4√p+5)`, so the source's weaker `(p−5)/16−(4√p+5)` is valid.

**Extensions and twisted terms.** For a character `ψ` of `Q` of exact order `d>1`, write its exponent on a generator of `Q` as `k₀=(n/d)t`, `gcd(t,d)=1`. The two full-field extensions have exponents `k₀` and `k₀+n` modulo `2n`. If `d` is even, both have order `2d`; if `d` is odd, exactly one does. Pick an order-`2d` extension `Ψ`; then `Ψ^d=χ`, the unique quadratic character. After expanding the four signs, a twisted term is `Ψ` of a rational product whose exponent at either simple root of `F` is `1` or `1+d` modulo `2d`. Neither vanishes for `d>1`, so no term is a `2d`th power. Replacing the negative exponents of `c` and `R` by nonnegative congruent exponents modulo `2d` changes values only at their excluded zeros and produces a polynomial with at most the same five geometric roots. The same general polynomial bound therefore gives `4√p+5` for each restricted term, without invoking a squarefree-order rational-function lemma. Averaging the 16 terms introduces **no factor of 16**: their coefficients are `±1/16`.

**Primitive sieve and threshold.** On the support of `P`, `χ(g)=χ(F)χ(c)χ(R)=+1`, and `c,R,F` are nonzero, so `g∈Q` and the exact cyclic primitive indicator applies. For each squarefree divisor `d|n`, its `φ(d)` characters appear with coefficient magnitude `1/φ(d)`; their aggregate contribution is at most one twisted bound. Including the principal coefficient gives exactly `W(n)=2^ω(n)`, yielding the source's lower bound `θ(n)[(p−5)/16−W(n)(4√p+5)]`. For `x=√p≥66W`, the multiplied bracket is at least `x²−64Wx−80W−5≥132W²−80W−5>0` for `W≥1`.

The explicit `p≥3,000,000,000` corollary is arithmetically valid. The 11 primes below 32 have product `200560490130`, and `2^55<12^5·200560490130`; primes at least 37 contribute no excess to `2^ω(n)/n^(1/5)`. Thus `W(n)<12n^(1/5)≤12(p/2)^(1/5)`. The inequality `√p≥792(p/2)^(1/5)` is equivalent to `4p³≥792^10`; it holds strictly at three billion and increases thereafter. This is a very loose but explicit universal sufficient cutoff.

**Graph implication.** With `R=c+a−1`, `F` as above, `b=ac/R`, and `L=F/R`, the four signs make `c,b,L` nonresidues and `R` a residue; `χ(1−b)=−χ(1−a)`. Also `U=R`, `V=L` have opposite signs, `g=L/c` generates `Q`, and `bR=ac`. Hence both internal rings are Hamiltonian and the two-edge switch in the bridge criterion works with `τ=a,M=c`. No extra phase or edge-disjointness condition is missing. This proves the specified one-short `K_(p,p+2)` page whenever the sieve finds a `c`.

My independent [parameter/full-edge checker](independent_one_cut_check.py) scanned all generators and `c` at primes through 401. It found restricted triples at `p=7,11` and all 73 primes from 17 through 401, none at 5 or 13, and separately reconstructed exact graph pages for 22 positive primes through 101. Its short cycle is built by cutting the two internal rings and inserting the two cross spokes; every edge appears once. A second [character/arithmetic checker](independent_character_audit.py) verifies the four-sign projector, extension order and `Ψ^d=χ` for every character at primes through 101, checks the twisted restricted sums numerically, and replays the exact integer cutoff comparisons. The finite checks corroborate the algebra; the unbounded claim relies on the Weil bound and sieve just audited. For a fixed small prime the chosen generator can lack a suitable `c` even when another generator works; the theorem's independence of `a` applies under its size condition.
