# Skeptical audit of the second-leaf shift-pair bridge

**Verdict: sound as an exact criterion for the specified two-substitution scheme, with no all-prime or graph-level conclusion.** I checked [`bridge.md`](bridge.md) against the parent shift-pair construction, reran its producer checker, and wrote a separate [residual-graph and exact-edge checker](independent_bridge_check.py). Its four literal pages and its fixed-base `p=7` negative claim pass.

For `p=2n+1`, every leaf of `K_(p,p+2)` has odd degree and therefore needs at least one singleton in any cycle/singleton partition. The proposed optimum has exactly `p+2` singleton edges, so it has **one per leaf**. After only the first `s→z` replacement, each old selected leaf `s∈Q` has three residual spokes (diagonal, short, and singleton), each old leaf outside `Q` has one, and `z` has one. Replacing a second old leaf in a full color frees two spokes there. A simple remaining short cycle can use at most two residual spokes at a leaf, leaving its one singleton; hence an old leaf's residual degree cannot exceed three. The second targets must therefore be distinct and outside `Q`, proving the stated injection `f:Q→F_p\Q`. A target in `Q` would have degree five, and a repeated outside target would also have degree five.

For an injection and distinct `w` neighbors, every full row is still a simple `2p`-cycle: it relabels two distinct leaf vertices of its original Hamilton row as `z,w`. The original rows are edge-disjoint; their new `z` spokes were distinct already, and the distinctness assumption on `(U_s,V_s)` makes their `w` spokes edge-disjoint. The residual graph `P` thus has degree three at each hub and at exactly the `2n=p−1` active old leaves `Q∪f(Q)`, and degree one at the remaining old leaf and at `z,w`. Any one remaining `2(p−1)`-cycle must use all active old leaves and `p−1` hubs, so it lies in the active subgraph. Conversely, such a simple cycle leaves one residual edge at every active leaf and the unique residual edge at each of the other three leaves. Those are exactly `p+2` distinct singleton spokes, and all graph edges are covered. This proves the stated **if and only if**, conditional on the injection and distinct `w` neighbors. The part count is optimal because the leaf-parity lower bound is `p+2+ceil((p(p+2)−(p+2))/(2p))=p+2+n+1`.

The inherited old short cycle already saturates every vertex in its `Q`-leaf/`Q`-hub component. Retaining all its edges therefore cannot yield one connected cycle through the new active leaves. Since a cycle crosses the boundary of that component an even number of times, at least two new cross spokes are needed; at least one old short edge is removed from the short cycle and becomes a singleton in this scheme. This is a method-specific obstruction to preserving the old short cycle, not an obstruction to `K_(p,p+2)`.

The multiplicative submodel's arithmetic also checks. With `f(s)=cs` for a nonresidue `c`, the `w` neighbors are `(c+a−1)s` and `(c+b−1)s`. They cover every nonzero hub exactly when these two coefficients are nonzero and have opposite quadratic characters. Then `0` is the omitted `w` hub; because the only residual hub-zero edges lead to the degree-one leaves `0,z,w`, the new short cycle also omits hub zero. The internal `Q` ring is one cycle because `a` generates `Q`; the internal nonresidue ring is one cycle when `L/c` generates `Q`. If `bR=Mτ` with `M∈{c,L}` and `τ∈{1,a}`, the proposed cuts at `(leaf 1,hub τ)` and `(leaf cτ/R,hub b)` are indeed internal edges, while the cross spokes `(leaf 1,hub b)` and `(leaf cτ/R,hub τ)` join their two opened paths into one cycle. This is sufficient, not necessary. At `p=13,a=4,b=6,c=5`, `L/c=12` has order two rather than six, while the literal bridge still succeeds; at `p=5`, neither multiplicative `c` works, but the nonmultiplicative injection does.

The independent checker reconstructs every original and transformed full cycle from the shift offsets, computes the entire residual edge set, confirms its degree pattern, verifies each saved short walk as one simple cycle, and counts every edge and singleton in `K_(p,p+2)`. It passes `p=5,7,11,13` with `35,63,143,195` edges respectively. The omitted `w`/short hubs are `(4,2)` at `p=5` and `(0,0)` in the other three controls. Independently enumerating all `4P3=24` injections for the fixed `p=7,a=2,b=6` base gives distinct-`w`-hub counts `{3:2,4:16,5:6}`; none reaches six. This excludes **that base in this scheme at the spoke stage**, while the separate `p=7,a=4,b=5` literal page is positive.
