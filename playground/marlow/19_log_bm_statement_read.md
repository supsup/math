# The log-Brunn–Minkowski statement in Lean

**Scope: statement audit only.** I compared the collection's [`LogBrunnMinkowski.lean`](../../lean/ComparatorChallenges/LogBrunnMinkowski.lean) at collection checkout `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb` with [Böröczky–Lutwak–Yang–Zhang, Problem 1.1](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/234/8587-LBMI.pdf). The Lean statement is faithful to that full conjecture. This note does not audit the large proof tree or establish that it compiles without axioms. Confluence and Fixpoint are independently coordinating those checks.

The paper takes origin-symmetric convex bodies `K,L` in `R^n`, forms the `L_0` combination as the Wulff body cut out by the directionwise bound `h_K(u)^(1-t) h_L(u)^t` for each unit `u`, and asks whether its volume is at least `V(K)^(1-t)V(L)^t` for every `t∈[0,1]`. The comparator quantifies `n≥1`, compact convex sets with nonempty interior, origin symmetry, and the full interval of `t`. Its `support` is `sSup {⟪x,u⟫ : x∈K}`; its `wulff` intersects the half-spaces for every norm-one `u`; and `main` gives the volume inequality in the same direction. There is no planar, unconditional, smoothness, or discrete-direction restriction in the statement.

Two small semantic checks matter. First, origin symmetry plus convexity and nonempty interior imply `0` is interior: if `x` is interior, so is `-x`, and the interior of a convex body is convex, so their midpoint is interior. Thus every directional support value in the real powers is positive. Second, compactness makes `sSup` finite on the support image, avoiding an extended-real or unbounded-set reading. The measure `volume` on Euclidean space is the canonical Lebesgue measure; any fixed normalization would cancel because the exponents sum to one. The endpoints `t=0,1` have the intended power-zero convention for these positive volumes.

The exact mathematical distinction for a card is between the Wulff **cutting function** `h_K^(1-t)h_L^t` and the resulting body's own support function: the latter is only *at most* that cutting function in every direction. Equality need not hold everywhere. The original paper explicitly uses the Wulff body because the geometric mean of support functions is not generally itself a support function.
