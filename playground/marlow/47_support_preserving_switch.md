# A two-color switch that preserves the odd-base schedule

The [long-cycle boundary](46_long_cycle_switching_boundary.md) asks for trades that connect prescribed 2-factors without changing their hub or leaf supports. A simple four-edge trade has exactly that property. It gives a local repair mechanism, but no proof that a suitable repair is always available.

Let `P,Q` be two edge-disjoint 2-regular color classes on their prescribed supports in the residual bipartite graph. Choose distinct hubs `u,v` and leaves `x,y` with `ux,vy∈P` and `uy,vx∈Q`. Exchange the four edge colors. Each of the four vertices loses and gains one edge of each color, so each class keeps degree two at every active vertex. Its support, the singleton spokes, the interval omission schedule, and the complete edge partition stay unchanged.

Suppose `ux` and `vy` lie in **different** `P` components. Removing one edge from each produces two paths; the exchanged edges join them into one cycle, so `P` loses a component. For `Q`, if its two removed edges lie in different components, `Q` also loses a component. If they lie in one cycle, removal leaves two paths. When their endpoint pairs are `(u,v)` and `(y,x)`, the new edges `ux,vy` join the paths and `Q` stays connected. When the pairs are `(u,x)` and `(v,y)`, each new edge closes its own path and `Q` splits. Thus the total number of components never increases in this orientation, and it strictly decreases unless `Q` splits.

The [checker](47_support_preserving_switch.py) gives a concrete repair using the saved `K_{9,11}` [certificate](23_nine_hub_seeds.json). In colors 0 and 1, start with the alternate valid 2-factorization obtained by trading `P` edges `(hub 0, leaf 6)` and `(hub 3, leaf 3)` against `Q` edges `(hub 0, leaf 3)` and `(hub 3, leaf 6)`. The resulting `P` has a 14-cycle and a 4-cycle, while `Q` remains an 18-cycle. Reversing the same trade merges `P` into one 18-cycle and leaves `Q` connected. The script verifies all 99 spokes, the original and traded supports, and the component lengths; it draws the [rectangle diagram](art/support_preserving_switch.svg). An independent audit repeated those edge and component checks.

This proves the local switch lemma and a nontrivial example, not the uniform odd-hub formula. A uniform argument would have to guarantee that whenever a scheduled color class is disconnected, some sequence of such trades can reduce the total component count without violating the prescribed supports. The local move alone does not guarantee that an improving rectangle is available.
